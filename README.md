# Prepare to Co-op Edition

Play all of **Dark Souls: Prepare to Die Edition** together with up to seven friends, from the Undead Asylum to the Kiln, without the limits of the game's own co-op. Guests keep their own body and progress, deaths respawn at a bonfire without bringing back the enemies the team has killed, a player who falls during a boss fight or an invasion spectates the others until the team wins or wipes (a wipe respawns everyone together in a world that starts over), boss kills and area changes no longer end the session, multiplayer fog walls are gone, a bonfire rest resets everyone's world (never in the middle of a teammate's boss fight), progress made together carries home, and enemies scale with the number of players. Evil spirits can invade a session too.

It is inspired by Yui's **Seamless Co-op** for Dark Souls Remastered. Nothing of that mod is used: its behaviour was studied and written again for Prepare to Die Edition's engine, with the same items, rules and session behaviour wherever the engine allows.

**Prepare to Co-op includes the DS1 FPS Unlock.** Its `xinput1_3.dll` is the [DS1 FPS Unlock](https://github.com/zofrea1/DS1-FPS-Unlock-PTDE-Remastered) for Prepare to Die Edition plus the co-op, built into one file, and its `PTDE-Coop-FPS-Unlock.ini` holds the settings of both. If you have Prepare to Co-op, you have the FPS Unlock: do not install both. The co-op works with the FPS unlock on or off (`FPSUnlock`).

## What's new in 1.8.0

- **Includes the DS1 PTDE FPS Unlock 2.2.0**: higher frame rates where the game's draw thread was the limit (Blighttown, Darkroot Garden, the Undead Burg), the same picture with less of its overhead; no more slowdown while standing still with fewer than four controllers; G-SYNC / FreeSync under DXVK. With Windows HDR on, NVIDIA's RTX HDR applies to the game only under DXVK and crushes the shadows: turn it off for the game in the NVIDIA app for the same picture as Direct3D 9.
- **The Parchment of Deliverance is retired**: no longer given at a bonfire, and one you carry is taken out of your inventory (one in the Bottomless Box goes when you take it out).
- **Plays with 1.5.0 to 1.7.0** (the same co-op protocol).

The full list is in the [1.8.0 release notes](release-notes/v1.8.0.md).

## What's new in 1.7.0

- **YOUR TEAM DIED for invaders**: when two or more invaders are in a world, whether they came as a group or one by one, and every one of them falls, they all see YOUR TEAM DIED, as a co-op team does after a wipe. (Fallen invaders already spectate the ones still standing until the invasion is decided.) An invader alone still sees YOU DIED.
- **A warning when a group invades**: the moment the world lets in the first player of a group invasion, before any of them has arrived, the host and every guest see "Beware! A band of N evil spirits is breaking into this world!", with how many are coming.
- **Spied on by the Death Knell**: when an invader's Death Knell watches a co-op player, that player sees "Your Death Knell tolls uneasily... an invader is spying on you!", at most once a minute however many invaders watch.
- **Level matching for invasions**: an invader's search prefers worlds with a player near the invader's soul level. It is a preference, not a limit: with few players a world far from your level can still be picked, a single world that can take you is invaded as before, and invasion cooldowns are kept. A world with a player in your level range (from 10% below your soul level to 20 + 10% above it, the original game's Red Eye Orb range) is 8 times as likely to be picked as one far from it; the pull fades with every level outside the range. A group invasion goes by its highest soul level.
- **Dried Fingers draw harder**: a world with Dried Fingers is now 150% more likely to be picked (was 65%), on top of the level matching.
- **Includes the DS1 PTDE FPS Unlock 2.1.0**: kicks, jumping attacks, roll direction and the switchback read the stick as at 30 FPS, Blighttown's swamp and lingering attacks such as Poison Mist build up at the original rate, and bodies and enemies are no longer flung when they load in.
- **Plays with 1.5.0, 1.5.1 and 1.6.0** (the same co-op protocol). Level matching needs 1.7.0 for the invader and for the world's host (a world of an older host counts as neither near nor far); YOUR TEAM DIED for invaders needs 1.7.0 for the invader only; the group warning needs 1.7.0 for the invaded world's host (and for each guest to see it); the Death Knell's warning needs 1.7.0 for the invader, the host and the player watched.

The full list is in the [1.7.0 release notes](release-notes/v1.7.0.md).

## Requirements

- Dark Souls: Prepare to Die Edition, the current Steam build (the same check as the FPS Unlock's).
- Steam running. While Prepare to Co-op is on, the game runs as Steam's Spacewar (app 480): Prepare to Die Edition's own Steam lobbies were switched off in 2022, and Spacewar is the long-standing community route for its multiplayer. Steam shows you as playing Spacewar.
- Everyone in a session uses the same `Password`, and Prepare to Co-op 1.5.0 or later: 1.5.0 changed the co-op protocol, and players of 1.0.0 to 1.4.0 are told to update when they try to join or invade a 1.5.0 world. Update together. 1.6.0 plays with 1.5.0; its group invasions need 1.6.0 for everyone in the group and for the world they invade.
- Works with DSfix (set `unlockFPS 0` in `DSfix.ini` if the FPS unlock is on).

## Install

1. From the zip on the [Releases](https://github.com/zofrea1/DS1-Prepare-to-Coop-PTDE/releases) page, copy `xinput1_3.dll` and `PTDE-Coop-FPS-Unlock.ini` next to `DARKSOULS.exe` (`dxvk_d3d9.dll` too, only for the FPS Unlock's experimental DXVK). If the FPS Unlock or an older Prepare to Co-op is already installed, these replace its files; set your settings again in the new INI, which has all of them.
2. In `PTDE-Coop-FPS-Unlock.ini`, set the same `Password` as your friends. `PrepareToCoop = true` is already set. (The older name `PTDE-FPS-Unlock.ini` is still read when there is no `PTDE-Coop-FPS-Unlock.ini`; if both are there, the one with Coop in its name is used and the other is ignored. File names are not case sensitive.)
3. Steam Deck and Linux: add the launch option `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="xinput1_3=n,b" %command%` so Proton loads the DLL (the same as for the FPS Unlock).
4. Your save: with Prepare to Co-op on, the game uses `DRAKS0005.co2` (`SaveFileExtension`) next to `DRAKS0005.sl2` in `Documents\NBGI\DarkSouls`. To play an existing character, copy `DRAKS0005.sl2` to `DRAKS0005.co2` before starting. Back up your saves.

The title screen reads PREPARE TO CO-OP EDITION while the co-op is on.

## How to play

Rest at any bonfire: each rest gives you every co-op item you do not carry.

| Crystal Eye Orb | Join the session of a player with your password. You arrive at the host's last bonfire, in your own body. |
| Chaos Eye Orb | Invade a session that allows invaders, as an evil spirit. Worlds with a player near your soul level are much likelier to be picked (1.7.0, see Invasions below). Defeat every co-op player for a Humanity each; fall, and each of them earns one. Used by a host with their world open (1.6.0), it takes the whole party into another world together, and brings it back to the host's world after (see Group invasions below); used again, it calls the search off. |
| Abyssal Eye Orb | A guest or an invader returns home; the host disbands the session. |
| Ominous Tome | Host: change the world rules (allies cannot harm each other, allies can harm each other, the host against the guests, free for all). |

They wither when anyone in the session rests at a bonfire, and the health they took comes back with the bar; a team wipe or the end of the session withers them too (a guest's as they arrive home, a host's when the host closes the world). |

They wither when anyone in the session rests at a bonfire or the whole team falls. |

DEBUFFED !"): a Crimson Blossom for you and a Bloody Mary for everyone (the enemies grow hardier), and, going to a co-operator, every soul and all humanity you carry, as the Darksign takes them. The items are used like any other item. Keyboard shortcuts do the same (`CoopHotkeys`): **Ctrl+F1** host, **Ctrl+F2** join, **Ctrl+F3** leave, **Ctrl+F4** invade. Another player's floating health bar shows their character's name with their soul level, ping and "lerp", the smoothing their character is drawn with (`OverheadPlayerDisplay`): everyone sees everyone's, invaders included, except a player under Chameleon, who has none. Joins, departures, deaths and rule changes are told at the bottom of the screen, by character name. If a player's connection drops (a crash, a lost network, a Steam Deck put to sleep), the others are told within seconds and that player is sent home.

Prepare to Co-op 1.5.0 changes the co-op protocol: its players play only with players of 1.5.0 or later, since its new enemy scaling, Bloody Mary and Death Knell need every game in the session to have them. (1.0.0 to 1.4.0 share the protocol before it and still play with each other.) Players who cannot play together are told so instead of "No sessions found": three times at the bottom of the screen, with the versions and who needs to update. An invader is told when every world it finds is of another version, and a host is told the same when such a player tries to join (from 1.1.0 on; Prepare to Co-op 1.0.0 itself only says "Failed to connect").

## Playing together

**Guests and the host's world.** Guests keep their own body (human or hollow), level, items and progress. They pick up items, get their own enemy drops and boss rewards, rest at bonfires, level up and talk to NPCs, who are real characters for them, not wandering ghosts. What an NPC says (in a conversation, or when hit or killed) is heard, with its subtitle, by every player near that NPC. A drop the world gives only once (a Black Knight's, a crystal lizard's, a Titanite Demon's, a mimic's, Pinwheel's masks, the Stray Demon's slab, a boss's rewards) reaches every player in the session: one whose game was loading, far away or down when it died gets their own roll a few seconds later (as does one whose game was loading when an NPC gave the party a gift), and such drops go straight into the inventory, so no death, team wipe or trip home can take one off the floor first. A slain NPC's loot needs none of this: it is laid out again at the NPC's spot on every load until each player has taken their own. Defeating a boss or crossing into another area no longer ends the session, joining is never locked, and players roam the host's world freely: the white fog walls that kept phantoms in the host's area are gone (boss fog stays). Every player's game runs the world's scripted events (`GuestWorldEvents`), so a guest exploring alone meets the Hellkite on the Undead Burg bridge, starts boss fights, rides lifts and gets the event rewards.

**Giving items.** An item you drop from your inventory (a weapon, a shield, armor, a ring, a soul: anything the game lets you drop for other players) lies on the floor in every co-op player's game, guests' and host's alike, and whoever picks it up first has it: it is gone from everyone else's floor (`ShareDroppedItems`). A player in another part of the world finds it when they get there. What the game never lets you drop (key items) stays as it is, and invaders neither see these drops nor drop any for the co-op players. A guest's pickup of a shared item waits a moment for the host's game, which decides who was first. Every player in the session needs 1.4.0.

**NPC summons.** A player in human form, the host or a guest, finds Solaire's sign and the other NPC signs and summons them; every player sees the phantom, and the NPC invaders (Kirk, Leeroy, Mildred, Jeremiah) come to guests too. A summon belongs to the world, not to whoever summoned it: it stays through anyone's death, rest or travel and when its summoner leaves, and goes home when it dies, on a team wipe, or when the host sends it home (while an NPC phantom is summoned, the host's Black Separation Crystal opens the game's own menu for it; a guest's crystal never dismisses an NPC).

**Travel.** Anyone can warp by bonfire, and everyone travels together to the same bonfire. A guest can warp to the bonfires the host's own warp could reach: the host owns the Lordvessel and has rested there. A player with a menu open follows once it is closed, and a player who has fallen follows once back on their feet. Nobody travels while a teammate who still stands fights a boss, a teammate is down, an invader is in the world, or the world is starting over after a lost boss fight. When anyone uses a Homeward Bone, the host or a guest, the host goes to their last bonfire and every guest follows a moment later; it takes one Homeward Bone from every co-op player, so it can be used only when everyone holds one, and as in single player it works in a boss fight, taking everyone out of it. The Homeward miracle, cast by the host or a guest, does the same whenever the bone could be used, and costs nobody a bone. The Darksign does the same, at the cost of everyone's souls and humanity, and it can always be used: in a boss fight, while a teammate is down, and during an invasion, where it sends the invaders home first. A guest who has fallen in a boss fight respawns at once and then follows. If the host is the one who has fallen, a guest's Darksign, or a guest's bone or miracle in a boss fight, still takes everyone, and the host respawns at once at that same bonfire. A guest on Prepare to Co-op 1.2.0 or older neither holds the bone back nor pays one, and loses no souls or humanity to the Darksign. A guest's Homeward Bone, Darksign or miracle takes everyone to the host's last bonfire too, and waits for the host to close any menu; both need Prepare to Co-op 1.3.0. None of these is a rest: the enemies stay as they were, as in single player.

**Bonfires.** Lighting a bonfire for the first time lights it for every player. In the host's world a guest's bonfires are the host's: lit where the host's are lit, kindled as far as the host's are (the same Estus at a rest), and a guest's Kindle kindles the host's bonfire. Back home the guest's own bonfires are as they left them, plus any they lit on the way (unkindled). When anyone rests, every player's world resets (enemies respawn), and a teammate who fell and is spectating respawns. Nobody can rest while a teammate who still stands fights a boss (the bonfire is out of use, as with enemies nearby), so a rest never resets a fight in progress. A player who sat down before the fight began stays seated and can still level up, attune, use the Bottomless Box, kindle or reverse hollowing; only the warp waits for the fight. The others see you sit down, rest, kindle, reverse hollowing and stand up (`BonfireAnimations`).

**Deaths.** You respawn at your last bonfire in the host's world (the host's, until you rest at one yourself). The session goes on, the host's death included, and every death is announced. A death keeps the world as it is: the enemies the team has killed since the last rest stay dead in your reload, as they are for everyone else, host or guest. Each death in a session also costs a Crimson Blossom (see the items above): the price of a death that keeps the world. Whatever brings the enemies back takes the Blossoms away: anyone's rest withers everyone's (no need for each player to rest), and so does a team wipe or a death with no teammate in the world. A guest leaves their bloodstain where they fell, in the host's world, and finds the same stain at the same place back in their own.

**Boss fights and invasions are fought as one team.** Fall during a boss fight (yours, or a teammate's anywhere in the world) or while an invader is in the world, with another co-op player still standing, and you do not die yet: the camera follows a living teammate in full colour, turned with your own stick (D-pad left/right or the arrow keys switch between teammates). Your game draws the world around that teammate and updates their character every frame, so a teammate far away is seen in full detail and moves smoothly. While you spectate, your game never takes an enemy over from a standing player. Your death ("YOU DIED", the lost souls, the respawn) goes ahead once the fight is decided:

- **The team wins** (the boss is gone and no invader is left): the fallen respawn at their bonfires, and the boss stays dead.
- **Everyone falls** (a team wipe): the death text says "YOUR TEAM DIED", each respawn waits after it until every player's death has got there, then every game reloads at once into a world that starts over, the boss and the enemies included, as after a death in single player. Every other death keeps "YOU DIED".
- **Someone rests at a bonfire**: the spectators respawn at once. If the boss was still awake in a game (a lost fight), that game reloads at its bonfire together with the fallen, so the boss starts over for everyone. The reload waits until the player who rested leaves the bonfire menu, and that player is told that the others are waiting.
- **Ten minutes** pass, as a last resort.

**Invasions.** With `AllowInvaders`, others can invade as evil spirits. An invader arrives at the game's own invasion points (the Undead Asylum and Firelink Shrine included), the multiplayer walls come down for them, and invaders, the host and the guests all see each other's health bars (Chameleon hides the caster's). While an invader is in the world, bonfires are out of use (and, with `SealBossFogsDuringInvasions`, boss fog stays shut for the host and the guests), and the Abyssal Eye Orb, Homeward Bone, Humanity, Twin Humanities, Divine Blessing, Elizabeth's Mushroom and Fire Keeper Souls cannot be used. The host's Darksign still can: the invaders are sent home (nobody wins), and the host and every guest go to the host's last bonfire, each losing all souls and humanity. Nobody joins or invades while anyone in the world is in a boss fight, and after an invasion ends the world takes no other invader for `InvasionCooldown` minutes. An invader who cannot get into any world is told why: a world on cooldown (with how long until it opens), a boss fight, or a full world. The invader wins only when every co-op player is down; every invasion has exactly one outcome. Invaders in the same world fight as one team: an invader who falls while another still stands spectates them, and they all win or lose together (the fallen stay meanwhile, so no new invader takes their place). When they lose, every one of them sees YOUR TEAM DIED (1.7.0). An invader's search picks among the worlds that can take them at random, weighted: a world with a player in the invader's level range (from 10% below their soul level to 20 + 10% above it) is 8 times as likely as one far from it, the pull fading with every level outside the range, and Dried Fingers make a world 150% more likely on top (1.7.0; a group goes by its highest soul level). It is a preference only: a world far from your level is still invaded when it is the only one, and cooldowns are kept. Invaders can drink from the Estus Flask, up to 5 charges per invasion: any more are set aside when they arrive and come back once they are home. With `InvasionRewards`, the winning side earns Humanity: the invader one per co-op player defeated (once home), or the host and every guest one each for every invader who falls, spectators included. A world takes at most as many invaders at once as it has co-op players, plus `ExtraInvaders` (one by default; 1.6.0): one player alone meets two invaders at most, a party of three four; `MaxPlayers` still counts everyone.

**Group invasions** (1.6.0). A host with their world open, with guests or alone, uses the Chaos Eye Orb (or Ctrl+F4) and the whole party invades another world together. The search runs while your world stays open, to co-op players and to invaders alike (a search is no shelter from invasion, for a host alone too), and looks for a world with room for all of you under its invader limit. That world's host holds your places for 45 seconds; places your group has not filled by then are open to any invader again. Only then does your world stop taking invaders, and it closes. Your guests go home first (a join always leaves from your own world) and every one of you invades that world, arriving as one team of evil spirits that wins or loses together. The world you invade is warned as your first player is let in (1.7.0): its host and guests see how many of you are coming. When the invasion is over and you are home, your world opens again and your guests are brought back to it, arriving at your last bonfire as any guest does. Use the orb again to call the search off. An invasion of your world comes first, so two parties after a fight have it: an invader who comes in during the search calls it off, and so does another group that your world holds places for, and neither lets a new search start until that invasion is over. It cannot start or go on during a boss fight, while the party travels, or while you or a guest of the group is down or on the way in either. Every guest in the group, and the world you invade, needs 1.6.0. A guest who comes in during the search stays out of the group and is sent home when your world closes.

**Progress.** The host's world is the session's: a guest arrives in it as the host has it, and nothing of the guest's own world comes along. From then on everyone progresses it together: what any player does there (a boss, a lever, an NPC's story) is shared with everyone. What your own game did there carries home with you (what you took part in), not what you only heard of from another player, and not the host's world as it was before you came. A boss that dies while you are in the world is dead in yours too, wherever you stood, and you get its souls and items (a unique item you already have stays the one you have). An NPC's story comes home a step at a time, and only while it lines up: a step your game saw goes home if that NPC stood at the same point in your world before it; your own quest stays where it was otherwise. Key items picked up in another world always reach your own save. In another world your own key items stay at home (the game's own rule), so you cannot open what the host's world has not opened yet; you do carry the keys you have that the host has too, and a key the host gets while you are there joins you at once if you have it at home. An NPC's fate (Oscar's, for one) follows the host's world and carries home with you. A disaster of the host's world (the black phantoms of black World Tendency or the Cursed Pendant) stays there.

**World rules.** The Ominous Tome makes co-op allies hostile to each other (everyone can hurt everyone, but nobody locks on to an ally), splits them into teams (the host against the guests), or makes it free for all (everyone fights everyone, and locks on to anyone; every player needs 1.4.0); each use moves on to the next, and after free for all back to none. Invaders are always hostile. The Cursed Pendant makes a world pure black World Tendency until it is used again, and Dried Fingers draws invaders to it.

**Enemies.** Every enemy gets more health, damage and poise per extra co-op player near it (`EnemyHealthScaling` ... `BossPostureScaling`; with `VicinityScaling`, on by default, a player counts for the enemies their game has active, so players who split up each fight enemies scaled for the players with them); bosses (every enemy with a boss health bar) use their own row, and the game's own extra boss health for co-op is left out. Each game scales its own copy of an enemy, so health bars drain correctly with extra players. Each enemy is moved by one game, normally the one whose player is nearest and whose copy of it is awake; a game far away, a sleeping copy, and a player who fell defer to a standing player nearby. An attack the enemy has begun plays to its natural end before another game takes it over, so a change of hands never cuts a swing short or turns it into another; targets, turning and the AI's choices stay the game's own. The host's scaling settings are used in every game of its world.

**Light.** Another player's Skull Lantern, Cast Light or Sunlight Maggot also lights your game.

**Finding a world.** If a world cannot take you (full, busy, mid-boss, gone), the next one found is tried. A host on the title screen is hidden from searches. A guest cannot join a host who is dead or in a boss fight; they are told to try again when the fight is over.

[docs/PREPARE-TO-COOP.md](docs/PREPARE-TO-COOP.md) describes what changes in a session in detail, how it works and how it differs from the Remastered mod.

## Settings

In the `[PrepareToCoop]` section of `PTDE-Coop-FPS-Unlock.ini` (the FPS Unlock's settings follow it in the same file; see [its README](https://github.com/zofrea1/DS1-FPS-Unlock-PTDE-Remastered#readme)).

| Key | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PrepareToCoop` | `true` | The co-op on or off. Off, the DLL is the FPS Unlock alone and the game is as usual (Steam app, save file, title screen). |
| `Password` | empty | Players with the same password find each other. Avoid `#` (it starts a comment). |
| `AllowInvaders` | `true` | Others may invade your session as evil spirits. |
| `ExtraInvaders` | `1` | How many invaders your world takes at once: as many as its co-op players (you and your guests) plus this many. 1 is the least (lower values count as 1), so a player alone meets two invaders at most and a party of three four; raise it to let larger groups of invaders in. `MaxPlayers` still holds for everyone together. The limit holds for invaders of every version; the searches of 1.6.0 pass over a world that has as many as it takes. |
| `InvasionRewards` | `true` | The side that wins an invasion gets Humanity: the invader one per co-op player defeated (once home), or the host and every guest one each when the invader falls. Both sides need 1.1.0 or later. |
| `InvasionCooldown` | `3` | After an invasion of your world ends (every invader has won, fallen, left or been sent home), no other invader can come in for this many minutes: 0 (no cooldown) to 10, fractions allowed (1.5 = 90 seconds). An invader's search passes over a world on cooldown and tries the others; when none can take them, they are told how long until the first one opens. The host's setting decides for its world. Invaders of 1.2.0 or older are just turned away. |
| `SealBossFogsDuringInvasions` | `false` | `true`: while an invader is in your world, boss fog stays shut for you and your guests, so a fight with an invader cannot be ended by walking into a boss. `false`: the fogs work as in the unmodded game: you can go in, the invader cannot follow, and your entry sends the invader home. The host's setting decides for its world. |
| `DeathDebuffs` | `true` | Each death in a session adds a Crimson Blossom (up to five; none when the team defeats a boss while you are down), each lowering maximum HP by 10% until anyone in the session rests at a bonfire (the health they took comes back), the whole team falls or the session ends. On by default since 1.1.0 (1.0.0's INI has it off). The Death Knell's price gives a Blossom either way. |
| `BloodyMary` | `true` | Each co-op player's death in a session gives everyone a Bloody Mary (up to five): the enemies gain a third of `EnemyHealthScaling` in health each, the bosses an eighth of `BossHealthScaling`, until anyone rests or the whole team falls. Either, both or neither of this and `DeathDebuffs`. The host's setting decides. The Death Knell's price gives one either way. |
| `GuestWorldEvents` | `true` | Guests run the world's scripted events in their own game, as the host does (bosses, lifts, cutscenes, the Hellkite). |
| `BonfireAnimations` | `true` | The others see you sit, rest and stand at a bonfire. |
| `SpectateStreaming` | `true` | Spectating a co-op player (after you fall in a boss fight, or with the Death Knell) loads their map when your game has not, and draws the world and the enemies around them as if you stood there. |
| `DeathKnellSoulLevel` | `false` | `true`: the Death Knell to a co-operator takes one soul level instead of every soul and all humanity (the Crimson Blossom and the Bloody Mary are its price either way). |
| `ShareDroppedItems` | `true` | An item a co-op player drops lies on every co-op player's floor, and the first to pick it up has it. The host's setting decides for the session. |
| `MaxPlayers` | `8` | How many players a session holds, the host, guests and invaders together, 2 to 8 (NPC summons do not count; 4 is the game's own number). A host lets a fifth player in only when every game in the session, the joiner's too, runs 1.4.0 and is set high enough. See [docs/MORE-PLAYERS.md](docs/MORE-PLAYERS.md). |
| `OverheadPlayerDisplay` | `5` | Another player's health bar: their character's name, with 0 nothing more, 1 ping, 2 soul level, 3 deaths, 4 soul level and ping, 5 soul level, ping and "lerp" (the smoothing their character is drawn with in your game; see `DynamicSmoothing`). The ping is your own round trip to that player, measured four times a second. |
| `NetworkTickRate` | `30` | How many times a second every game of your session sends where its player is (and the enemies it moves): 6, the game's own, up to 60. 30 is the default and a big improvement over the game's 6; 60 is viable and recommended when everyone in the session has a low-latency or excellent connection. Higher draws the others closer to where they really are, for more upload (about 2 KB a second to each other player at 30, twice that at 60). On an uneven connection raise `NetworkSmoothing` rather than lowering this. The host's setting is used by the whole session (players of 1.2.0 or older still send 6). |
| `NetworkSmoothing` | `33` | Above 6 updates a second, how many ms other players' characters (and enemies other games move) stay behind their updates, as a margin for late ones: they are drawn about one update interval plus this behind, plus the ping (about 66 ms at 30 a second, 50 ms at 60, 100 ms at 15; 6 a second stays the game's 200 ms). 33 ms is about one frame at 30 FPS or two at 60 FPS, enough to hide most ping spikes. Each player's own, 0 to 200; raise it if others move in jerks. With `DynamicSmoothing` on, this is only where each character's smoothing starts. |
| `DynamicSmoothing` | `true` | Above 6 updates a second, every other player's character and every enemy another game moves gets its own smoothing from how evenly its updates arrive: down to 25 ms on a steady connection, up to 200 ms on a poor one, rising quickly when updates come late and falling back once they are steady. Only the network counts, not your own frame rate. Shown as "lerp" on the health bars. `false`: `NetworkSmoothing` for everyone, always. |
| `VicinityScaling` | `true` | Each enemy is scaled for the co-op players near it (whose game has it active, the test that decides who gets a kill's souls), not for everyone in the session: two players in the Undead Burg and two in the Catacombs each fight enemies scaled for two. An enemy keeps its share of health when its scaling changes. `false`: every enemy for the whole session, as before 1.5.0. The host's setting decides. |
| `EnemyHealthScaling`, `EnemyDamageScaling`, `EnemyPostureScaling` | `50`, `0`, `35` | Enemy scaling per extra player, in percent. The host's settings are used by everyone in its world (guests of 1.3.0 or later). |
| `BossHealthScaling`, `BossDamageScaling`, `BossPostureScaling` | `150`, `0`, `40` | The same for bosses. |
| `SaveFileExtension` | `co2` | The save file while the co-op is on (vanilla: `sl2`). |
| `CoopHotkeys` | `true` | The Ctrl+F1..F4 shortcuts. |
| `CheckForUpdates` | `true` | At each start, look on the GitHub Releases page for a newer Prepare to Co-op, and say so on the title screen when there is one (until a character is loaded). One small request to api.github.com; `false` turns it off. |
| `UploadLogs`, `UploadLogsTo`, `UploadLogsName` | `false`, empty, empty | Send your game's log (`PTDE-FPS-Unlock.log`) to Prepare to Co-op's developer to help find and fix problems, when the developer asks you to: it goes up when the game closes, and a log a crash left behind goes at the next start. `UploadLogsTo` is the address the developer gives you (`https://user:password@host/folder/`); its user and password are the developer's upload account, not yours, and can only add logs to that folder (not see, read or delete anything). `UploadLogsName` is the name your logs are filed under, so the developer knows whose they are (letters, digits, `-` and `_`); empty, this PC's name. Off by default, and nothing is sent without both `UploadLogs = true` and an address. The log holds your Steam name and id and those of the players you met, your characters' names and the PC's name. |

## Notes and known limits

- Up to eight players (`MaxPlayers`). Above four, every player in the session needs 1.4.0 and the setting high enough; each game sends to every other, so a full session takes more upload; with seven others on the health bars (the HUD has seven) an NPC summon has none.
- **Online play: no guarantees, use at your own risk.** The mod changes the game in memory while it runs and runs the game under Spacewar. No responsibility is accepted for bans, lost progress or anything else that results from using it.
- Some cutscenes that move a player into another map have only been checked for the common ones (the Undead Asylum's crow, the Painted World).
- An enemy killed in the last moment of a fallen player's load can come back in that player's game.
- Spectating a teammate far away with `SpectateStreaming` off: their character and their surroundings are drawn in full, but the world more than about 25 to 30 m from them can be missing.
- While spectating, lock-on only reaches enemies near where you fell, not near the teammate you watch.
- The first invasion of a world after an earlier invasion of it can fall through while connecting. The mod now knocks once more by itself ("The connection fell through. Trying again..."); in testing, a second try always got in.

## Logs and bug reports

Everything goes to the FPS Unlock's log, `PTDE-FPS-Unlock.log` next to the DLL; the co-op's lines start with `[coop]`. A crash writes `PTDE-FPS-Unlock-crash.dmp`. Send the logs of every player in the session, and any dump, with a bug report.

## Build

Visual Studio 2022 (MSVC). The FPS Unlock is a git submodule (`fps-unlock`, pinned to the release it ships with):

```
git clone --recursive https://github.com/zofrea1/DS1-Prepare-to-Coop-PTDE.git
cd DS1-Prepare-to-Coop-PTDE
build.bat
```

`build.bat` compiles the FPS Unlock's sources (`fps-unlock\src\ptde\sources.txt`) together with this repository's (`src\sources.txt`) into `build\xinput1_3.dll`, writes `build\PTDE-Coop-FPS-Unlock.ini` (`PrepareToCoop.ini` followed by the FPS Unlock's INI) and fetches DXVK through the FPS Unlock's script. The co-op plugs into the FPS Unlock's start-up through `fps-unlock\src\ptde\extension.h`; `src\extension.cpp` replaces its `extension_none.cpp`. `package_release.bat` makes the release zip. To move to a newer FPS Unlock, check out its release in `fps-unlock`, rebuild and commit the submodule.

`tools\build_title_movie.bat` remakes the title movie from the game's own and `assets\PrepareToCoop-Title.png`. It also needs the game's original title texture (the 512 x 512 `Title` texture in `menu/english/menu_local.tpf`, saved as a PNG) as `assets\Title-original-PTDE.png`; that file is the game's own and is not in the repository. `tools\embed_title.py` remakes `src\title_art.inc` from the PNG.
