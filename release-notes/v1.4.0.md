# Prepare to Co-op Edition v1.4.0

Up to eight players, smoothing that follows each player's connection, items you can hand to each other, guests who summon NPCs, a host's world that only the session's own play changes, and boss souls and one-time drops that reach every player wherever they stood. 1.4.0 plays with 1.0.0 to 1.3.0 (same protocol); what is new needs the players involved on 1.4.0, so update together.

**Includes the [DS1 PTDE FPS Unlock](https://github.com/zofrea1/DS1-FPS-Unlock-PTDE-Remastered) 1.9.0**, as 1.3.0 did.

## What's new

### Players and sessions
- **Up to eight players** (`MaxPlayers`, 8 by default): the host, guests and invaders together, NPC summons on top, tested with eight. Above four, every player in the session needs 1.4.0 and `MaxPlayers` set high enough. Two limits of the game's own networking are raised with it (its sessions had room for five people, its network memory for about six). Every other player gets a floating health bar (the game's HUD has seven), players before NPC summons. See [docs/MORE-PLAYERS.md](https://github.com/zofrea1/DS1-Prepare-to-Coop-PTDE/blob/main/docs/MORE-PLAYERS.md).
- **Give each other items** (`ShareDroppedItems`, on by default). An item you drop (a weapon, armor, a ring, a soul: anything the game lets you drop) lies on every co-op player's floor, and the first to pick it up has it. Invaders neither see these drops nor make any. Every player in the session needs 1.4.0.
- **Guests can summon NPCs.** A guest in human form finds Solaire's sign and the other NPC signs and summons them as the host does, and the NPC invaders (Kirk, Leeroy, Mildred, Jeremiah) come to guests too.
- **NPC summons belong to the world.** Whoever summoned him, Solaire stays through anyone's death, rest or travel and when his summoner leaves. He goes home when he dies, on a team wipe, or when the host sends him home: while an NPC phantom is summoned, the host's Black Separation Crystal opens the game's own menu for it.
- **Free for all** (Ominous Tome): a fourth world rule in which every co-op player can hurt and lock on to every other. Every player needs 1.4.0.
- **Invaders** can go home with the Abyssal Eye Orb, and walk, run and sprint on the others' screens as co-op players do.
- **Dried Fingers** make a world 65% more likely to be an invader's pick (50% before), and there is no invasion cooldown while they are in effect.

### Network
- **Dynamic smoothing** (`DynamicSmoothing`, on by default): every other player's character, and every enemy another game moves, gets its own smoothing from how evenly its updates arrive, from 25 ms on a steady connection up to 200 ms on a poor one. The floating health bar shows it as "lerp" (`OverheadPlayerDisplay = 5`, the new default).
- **A fresh ping**: the ping on a health bar is your own round trip to that player, measured four times a second.
- **Fast enemies stay smooth at high tick rates** (the Taurus Demon down the bridge runs instead of sliding), and movement is smoother on good connections.

### World
- **The host's world is the host's.** A joining guest's game sends nothing until its copy of the host's world is the host's, so a guest's own progress can no longer play out in the host's world (a joiner's Lautrec killed the Firekeeper in two worlds in testing).
- **Only what you did comes home.** Your own world takes what your game did in the host's world, not what you only heard of from another player. An NPC's quest comes home only while it lines up with where that NPC stands in your world.
- **A boss counts for everyone in the world**, wherever they stood: dead in their world too, with its souls and items. The Stray Demon's defeat reaches every player.
- **One-time drops for players elsewhere.** A crystal lizard or Black Knight killed in a map nobody else is in gives everyone their drop within seconds.
- **Guests see the host's bonfires**, lit and kindled as the host's, and a guest's Kindle kindles the host's bonfire. Back home a guest's own bonfires are as they left them.
- **Scripted set pieces play for the player who set them off**: the Hellkite's first flight over the bridge no longer goes missing when the host is far away, and a boss's scripted leap is never handed to another game halfway through.
- **The lifts between Firelink Shrine and the Undead Parish, and Firelink and New Londo**, ride for everyone when called from either end.
- **Enemy scaling comes down as players leave**, also for enemies far away.
- The game's own C++ runtime errors and network errors are logged, a runtime error with a dump.

## Fixes

- **Guests saw no NPC summon signs**: the game allowed a guest no kind of sign at all, whatever the world's state.
- **A Homeward Bone or Darksign trip left a player behind**: a message without buttons (a locked door's) counted as an open menu long after it was gone.
- **Players paired with the wrong character** after a reload: names on the wrong health bars, a living player rebuilt, the fallen spectating someone already dead. Characters are matched by Steam id now.
- **A living player could vanish** from one player's screen after an update that said they had no health.
- **A boss in two places at once**, enemies passed back and forth between games every second, and sleeping bosses changing hands all session.
- **Bosses turning away in mid-swing**, and snapping around when they changed hands.
- **Enemies that hung back** when a guest walked up to them alone (the ambushing hollows near Firelink lost their jump).
- **The host earned souls for kills far away**; a kill now pays in the games that run the enemy.
- **Enemies sliding** at high frame rates, a full walk shown as a run, and a sprint shown with no stamina.
- **The Cursed Pendant's NPC invaders** never came to a hollow player.
- **A fallen player** missed a floor drop like the Stray Demon's Titanite Slab; it now comes as "From a kill you missed".
- **A guest sent to the wrong bonfire** after sitting down at their own during the join, and a free kindle when a guest lit a bonfire the host had lit.
- **An invasion that waited 40 seconds for nothing** when the world's player fell as the invader came.
