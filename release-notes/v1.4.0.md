# Prepare to Co-op Edition v1.4.0 (draft)

Smoothing that follows each player's connection, scripted set pieces that every player sees, fast enemies that stay
smooth at high tick rates, and the first step to sessions of up to eight players. 1.4.0 plays with 1.0.0 to 1.3.0
(same protocol); what is new needs the players involved on 1.4.0.

## What's new

### Network
- **Dynamic smoothing** (`DynamicSmoothing`, on by default). Every other player's character, and every enemy another
  player's game moves, gets its own smoothing from how evenly its updates arrive: down to 16 ms on a steady connection,
  up to 200 ms on a poor one (players across the world, 200 ms pings, Wi-Fi). It rises quickly when updates start
  coming late and falls back within a few seconds once they are steady again; only the network counts, not your own
  game's frame rate or stalls. `NetworkSmoothing` is where it starts; `DynamicSmoothing = false` keeps
  `NetworkSmoothing` for everyone.
- **The floating health bar shows it**: `OverheadPlayerDisplay = 5` (the new default) adds "lerp 48ms", the smoothing
  that player is drawn with in your game, after the ping.
- **Fast enemies stay smooth at high tick rates.** An enemy's copy is drawn a little behind its updates on purpose;
  the test that makes a copy jump to an update now allows for that, so the fastest movers (the Taurus Demon bounding
  down the bridge at 60 updates a second) run instead of sliding.

### World
- **Scripted set pieces play for the player who set them off.** A flight, leap or landing an event plays on an enemy
  is now moved by the game of the player whose step began it, until it ends, and every other game follows. The
  Hellkite's first flight over the Undead Burg's bridge no longer goes missing when the host is far away, and a boss's
  scripted leap is never handed to another game halfway through.
- **No more sudden turns when a boss changes hands.** Since 1.3.0 a boss that passed to another player's game was
  turned on that player at once, which made bosses snap around in fights with nothing wrong. A boss now keeps the
  target its own AI chose; only one that has had no target for 4 seconds while a player stands near it in its fight
  (the second Bell Gargoyle after the host fell) is pointed at that player.

### Players
- **Co-op players can give each other items** (`ShareDroppedItems`, on by default). An item you drop from your
  inventory (a weapon, a shield, armor, a ring, a soul: anything the game lets you drop for other players) lies on
  every co-op player's floor, the host's and the guests', and the first to pick it up has it: it is gone from every
  other floor. A player elsewhere in the world finds it when they get there. Key items still cannot be dropped, and
  invaders neither see these drops nor drop any for the co-op players. A guest's pickup of a shared item waits a
  moment for the host's game, which decides who was first. Every player in the session needs 1.4.0 (with an older
  host a guest's drops stay their own, as before).
- **Guests can summon NPCs.** A co-op guest in human form finds Solaire's sign and the other NPC signs and summons
  them as the host does; every other player sees the phantom. The NPC invaders (Kirk, Leeroy, Mildred, Jeremiah)
  now come to a guest who walks into their area, too. The host needs 1.4.0 for the other players to see a guest's
  summon in every case.
- **NPC summons belong to the world.** Whoever summoned him, Solaire stays through anyone's death, rest or travel,
  the host's included, and when his summoner leaves. He goes home when he dies, on a team wipe, or when the host
  sends him home with the Black Separation Crystal: while a white NPC phantom is summoned, the host's crystal opens
  the game's own menu to send phantoms home (otherwise it closes the world, as before). A guest's crystal still takes
  them home and never dismisses an NPC.
- **Invaders can use the Abyssal Eye Orb** to return home, as the Black Separation Crystal already did.
- **Dried Fingers draw invaders harder.** A world with Dried Fingers is 65% more likely to be an invader's pick (50%
  before; the invader's game picks, so this needs the invader on 1.4.0), and while they are in effect there is no
  invasion cooldown: using them ends one that is running, and an invasion that ends before the session does starts
  none. The next session has the host's `InvasionCooldown` again (the host needs 1.4.0).
- **The Cursed Pendant brings the NPC invaders to hollow players.** The curse took a hollow player for human, but the
  game threw away the invader's sign: no NPC invader came to a hollow player in a cursed world. They come now, to the
  host and to guests.
- **Up to eight players** (`MaxPlayers`, experimental, 4 by default): the host, guests and invaders together, NPC
  summons on top. Above four every player in the session needs 1.4.0 and `MaxPlayers` set high enough. Every other
  player gets a floating health bar (the game's HUD has seven), and every player before any NPC summon: an NPC gets
  a bar when one is left (at four players too, where a fourth player used to have none next to Solaire). See
  docs/MORE-PLAYERS.md.
- **The party list holds eight** (at any `MaxPlayers`): the game's own list of who is in the world, which runs the
  summons (their arrival, death and departure, the Black Separation Crystal's menu), had four places. Four players and
  Solaire, or Solaire with five or more players, now each have theirs, and NPC signs and NPC invasions work while
  there is room in it, also with four players or more.

## Fixed

Found in a five-player test (2026-10-31); most of it can happen with fewer players.
- **Players paired with the wrong character.** Each game matches the other players' characters to their players.
  After everyone came back at one bonfire (a reload), three of five games matched them by where they stood and got
  some wrong: names on the wrong health bars, a living player's character rebuilt when another player fell, and
  fallen players spectating someone already dead ("Waiting for the fight to be decided" with a teammate still
  standing). The match now comes from the game itself: each character the game made for a session member, and
  that member's Steam id, which is exact whatever the names (two players with the same Steam or character name
  included).
- **A boss in two places at once.** A game the host stopped handing enemies to kept moving the ones it had: the
  Asylum Demon was fought by two games at once, and a player whose game got both saw it jump between two places
  and two attacks. Such a game now lets go of an enemy as soon as another game moves it. A boss whose copy stopped
  getting updates mid-attack also no longer blocks every other game from taking it for 20 seconds.
- **A living player vanishing from one player's screen.** An update that said a player had no health made that
  player die in that one game, invisibly, while they played on. A player's death now waits for their own game to
  say so (at most 1.5 seconds).
- **Smoothing that jumped around** on the floating health bars of a slower machine: its own frame stalls counted as
  network delay for everyone at once.

Found in a second five-player test (2026-11-01), with players spread over Firelink Shrine and the Undead Burg:
- **The host earned souls for kills far away.** The host's game takes every player's hits on the enemies it still
  has, also ones it no longer runs because no one is near them in that game, so a guest's kill in the Undead Burg
  paid the host at Firelink, while the guests at Firelink got nothing. A kill now pays only in a game that runs the
  enemy, for the host as for the guests (the Ring of the Evil Eye's heal too). The drops are not changed.
- **Enemies sliding** in other players' games at high frame rates: a copy of an enemy another game moves picks its
  walk or run from its speed, which the game worked out for 30 FPS, so above about 60 FPS a running enemy was drawn
  walking while it moved at a run. It now walks and runs by its real speed at any frame rate (as other players'
  characters already did).

Found in a third five-player test (2026-11-01, evening):
- **An invasion that waited 40 seconds for nothing.** When the world's player fell just as the invader came, the
  game called the invasion off within seconds, but the invader then waited out the whole 40 s join time before
  "Failed to connect". Now the invader asks that world again at once (it answers that its player is down) and then
  tries the next world. A join that runs out of time no longer leaves the host's bonfire as the player's own last one.
- **Enemies that hung back** when a guest walked up to them alone: the host's game, far away, still held them, and they
  stood still until the guest's game next reported its bids (every 5 s) and the host's next handed enemies out (every
  3 s). The armored hollows that ambush you by the sewer from Firelink Shrine often lost their jump that way: by the
  time the guest's game had them, the player was already past their trigger. Both now go every second.
- **Other players' characters that walked, stopped and walked on.** Dynamic smoothing followed the average
  unevenness of a player's updates, which on a good connection is tiny, and stayed at 16 ms; the slightly late
  updates that come once or twice a second on any connection then left the character standing until the next one.
  The smoothing now also covers the latest update of the last few seconds (up to twice the gap between updates), so
  expect 25 to 35 ms on the floating health bars where it showed 16.
- **A guest sent to the wrong bonfire.** A guest who sat down at their own bonfire while their join was still loading
  had that bonfire as their respawn in the host's world, and their next death took them to it there (the Undead
  Asylum instead of the host's bonfire in the Undead Burg).
